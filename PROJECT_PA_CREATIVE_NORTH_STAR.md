# PROJECT_PA Creative North Star

Last updated: 2026-06-21

## Purpose

This document is the highest creative direction for Project_PA.

Future design, code, scene, UI, backlog, migration, and demo work must follow this document first. `PROJECT_PA_DESIGN_INTENT.md` remains important, but its reverse supply-chain management concept should now be interpreted inside the broader cozy day-to-night life and shop simulation described here.

## One-Line Definition

Project_PA is a cozy 3D life and shop management simulation where the player spends the day exploring, gathering, fishing, farming, mining, decorating, and preparing goods, then opens a personal shop at night; the products the player handles and sells gradually change the village economy and culture.

## Core Fantasy

The player is not only a passive manager and not only a shopkeeper.

The player fantasy is:

- Live in a warm low-poly island village.
- Walk around, gather resources, meet residents, improve the town, and prepare goods during the day.
- Open and operate a small shop at night.
- Decide what to display, how to price it, and how to respond to customer demand.
- Watch the village change because of the categories, quality, and culture of the goods that pass through the shop.
- Grow from a small personal stall into the center of a living local economy.

## Three Game Promises

1. Daytime is cozy village life.
   - The player explores, collects, fishes, mines, farms, decorates, talks to NPCs, and prepares stock.
   - These actions should feel tactile and personal, not like pure spreadsheet work.

2. Nighttime is player-run shop operation.
   - The player opens the shop, stocks displays, sets prices, reads customer reactions, closes the day, and reviews settlement results.
   - The shop phase should feel like the player is hosting the village economy.

3. What the player sells changes the village.
   - Food, materials, crafted goods, cultural items, tools, and premium products should push the town in different directions.
   - Sales should influence demand, resident behavior, facility unlocks, reputation, events, and long-term identity.

## Day-To-Night Growth Loop

The core long-term loop should become:

1. Morning planning: check money, village needs, shop stock, requests, and growth goals.
2. Day activity: explore, gather, fish, mine, farm, decorate, talk, buy from producers, or prepare goods.
3. Preparation: sort inventory, process goods, choose display items, and decide shop strategy.
4. Night shop: open the shop, stock displays, price items, observe customers, handle sales and rejection feedback.
5. Settlement: review revenue, demand, village category progress, audit/tier/reputation, and next-day changes.
6. Growth: unlock facilities, NPC support, decoration options, new product categories, and automation that reduces repetitive work.

## Player Role

The player should feel like a resident-owner-operator.

The player can personally do cozy life-sim actions, especially early in the game:

- gather small resources
- fish
- mine
- farm
- decorate
- talk to residents
- choose stock
- run the counter

But the long-term arc should move from doing everything manually to building a village economy where NPCs and facilities support production, logistics, processing, and sales.

## Shop Operation Fantasy

The shop is the emotional and mechanical center of Project_PA.

It should not be only a pretty storefront. It should feel like:

- a place the player owns
- a night market stage
- the point where daytime effort becomes revenue
- a readable interface for product identity, price, demand, and reputation
- the social place where NPC tastes and town needs become visible
- the hub through which the village changes

## Village-Change Principle

Every major product category should eventually have a visible village effect.

Examples:

- Food sales can improve daily mood, visitor flow, and cooking-related facilities.
- Material sales can support construction, repairs, workshops, and infrastructure.
- Crafted/cultural goods can improve town personality, decoration, tourism, and resident pride.
- Tools and processed goods can improve productivity, NPC work efficiency, and automation.
- Premium goods can increase reputation, audits, specialist interest, and higher-tier customers.

This is the main rule: selling an item should not only make money; it should say what kind of village the player is building.

## Reverse Supply-Chain Reinterpretation

The previous reverse supply-chain identity is preserved, but it is no longer the only surface fantasy.

It should be treated as the systemic backbone behind the cozy life/shop loop:

- Early game: the player personally explores and prepares enough goods to understand the world.
- Mid game: NPC producers, specialists, and facilities reduce repetitive labor.
- Late game: the player manages a wider supply network, product identity, town direction, automation, and reputation.

NPC production should support the life-sim experience, not erase it. The goal is not to prevent the player from gathering forever; the goal is to make manual activity meaningful early and optional/strategic later.

## NPC Roles

NPCs should support both cozy life and economic simulation.

They can be:

- neighbors with schedules, tastes, relationships, and dialogue
- customers with budgets, preferences, and feedback
- producers who bring resources into the economy
- specialists who process goods or unlock recipes
- helpers who automate repetitive tasks
- cultural signals showing what the village is becoming

NPC systems should make the town feel alive, not only efficient.

## Tier, Facility, And Event Unlock Principles

Progression should be driven by a combination of:

- revenue
- product categories sold
- resident satisfaction
- relationship growth
- facility usage
- village identity direction
- audit/reputation results

Unlocks should not feel like pure corporate approval. They should feel like the village gaining confidence, culture, infrastructure, and economic depth.

## Art, Sound, And UI Direction

The presentation target is cozy, bright, low-poly, readable, and warm.

Art should show:

- a welcoming village
- clear day/night mood changes
- readable shop displays
- product categories at a glance
- cozy life activity spaces
- visible town growth

UI should act as a friendly management cockpit:

- day/time
- money
- current stock
- shop open/closed state
- customer reactions
- village category progress
- next unlock
- daily settlement

UI should help the player make decisions without turning the game into a dry business dashboard.

## What Not To Do

- Do not make Project_PA a clone of any commercial game.
- Do not copy commercial names, logos, UI layouts, or exact systems.
- Do not treat Project_D as a merge source.
- Do not turn Project_PA into a pure shop-only game.
- Do not turn Project_PA into a pure passive manager game where the player never touches the world.
- Do not erase existing reverse supply-chain systems; reinterpret them as growth/automation/support systems.
- Do not make cozy visuals the whole goal while leaving the loop shallow.
- Do not add broad systems before the day-to-night shop loop is understandable.

## MVP, Submission Demo, And 1.0

### MVP

The MVP proves the day-to-night loop:

- two or more daytime activities
- stock preparation
- shop opening and closing
- item display and price setting
- customers with buy/reject feedback
- daily settlement
- one visible village-change signal

### Graduation/Submission Demo

The submission demo can be a stable slice of the MVP or first-week loop. It should be polished enough for evaluation, but it does not need the full 1.0 breadth.

### Long-Term 1.0

The 1.0 goal is a repeatable cozy life and shop management game with multi-week progression, village change, facilities, NPC support, relationships, product categories, save/load, and long-term growth.

## Milestone Structure

### Milestone 1: Cozy Day-to-Night Shop Loop

Goal: prove the core identity.

Required:

- Day phase with at least two activities such as gathering, fishing, mining, farming, decorating, talking, buying supply, or processing.
- Stock information and inventory preparation.
- Night shop opening/closing.
- Product display and pricing.
- At least two customer types.
- Buy/reject feedback.
- Revenue and daily settlement.
- Next-day change or unlock.
- At least one product category that affects village change.

### Milestone 2: Village Culture Growth

Goal: make sold items visibly shape the town.

Required:

- category-based village progress
- resident demand trends
- visible town/facility changes
- relationship and reputation feedback

### Milestone 3: Expansion, Specialists, And Seasonal Life

Goal: make the town support longer play.

Required:

- shop expansion
- storage/logistics
- specialist NPC support
- stronger processing chains
- seasonal or weekly demand shifts

### Milestone 4: Long-Term Systems

Goal: make Project_PA durable as a full game.

Required:

- robust save/load
- broader balancing
- late-tier unlocks
- long-play QA
- build stability
- future demo snapshots from the full game

## Future Codex Priorities

1. Preserve this Creative North Star.
2. Build the day-to-night loop before adding more management complexity.
3. Keep Day 1 route safe while expanding Day 2+ into actual life/shop play.
4. Add daytime activities that produce meaningful shop stock.
5. Add shop open/close structure and daily settlement.
6. Make product categories drive village changes.
7. Reuse existing shop, economy, inventory, NPC, tier, audit, and save systems incrementally.
8. Treat Project_D and VisualTargets only as visual/readability references.
